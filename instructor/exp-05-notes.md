# Instructor Notes — Experiment 5

## Prep (30 min)

- Strip the reflective layer from one CD to give a transmission grating, and
  keep intact discs for the reflection measurement. A Blu-ray disc is worth
  having even though (in fact *because*) it produces no first order at
  $650\ \text{nm}$.
- Set up the two-pinhole target for Part E at a fixed, measured distance and
  leave it there for all groups, so their resolution numbers are comparable.
- Check the rotation stage's vernier and its zero.

## Expected results

- $a = 0.08\ \text{mm}$, $\lambda = 650\ \text{nm}$: first minimum at
  $0.466°$; central maximum $32.5\ \text{mm}$ wide at $L = 2.00\ \text{m}$.
- $600\ \text{l/mm}$ grating: first order at $22.95°$; maximum observable
  order is $m = 2$.
- CD ($1.60\ \mu\text{m}$): first order at $23.97°$.
  DVD ($0.740\ \mu\text{m}$): first order at $61.4°$ — **small-angle
  approximation fails badly**, and this is the pedagogical point of including
  the DVD.
  Blu-ray ($0.32\ \mu\text{m}$): $\lambda/d = 2.03 > 1$, so no first order
  exists. Groups should record the absence and turn it into a bound.
- Grating resolving power: $1000\ \text{l/mm}$ over $5\ \text{mm}$ gives
  $R = 5000$ in first order, i.e. $0.13\ \text{nm}$ at $650\ \text{nm}$.
  Sodium D separation needs $R = 987$ — comfortably achievable, which matters
  because Week 11 depends on it.
- Eye resolution: $1.22 \times 550\ \text{nm}/6\ \text{mm} = 112\ \mu\text{rad}$,
  i.e. $0.11\ \text{m}$ at $1\ \text{km}$. Headlights are $\sim1.4\ \text{m}$
  apart, so they are resolvable at 1 km — students often expect otherwise.
- Oil-immersion microscope, NA 1.4, $550\ \text{nm}$: $196\ \text{nm}$.
  $100\ \text{keV}$ electron: $\lambda_{\text{dB}} = 3.70\ \text{pm}$
  (relativistic; $3.88\ \text{pm}$ non-relativistically) — five orders of
  magnitude better, which answers question 8.

## Where groups get stuck

1. **Grating not at normal incidence.** Check by comparing $|\theta_{+1}|$
   and $|\theta_{-1}|$ — the handout says to, but few do unprompted.
2. **Using small angles for the DVD.** Gives $d$ wrong by nearly a factor of
   two. Watch for it.
3. **Measuring only $m = \pm1$** for the single slit. Insist on $\pm4$.
4. **Part E variance between observers.** This is real and is the point; do
   not let them treat one observer's threshold as exact.

## Grading notes

- Question 7 (grating resolving power versus the sodium doublet) is a
  prerequisite check for Week 11. If a group gets it wrong, catch it now.
- The Blu-ray non-observation should be reported as a bound, not as a failure.
- Watch for the $1.22$ factor being dropped in the Airy analysis.

## Safety

Discs send several orders in different directions simultaneously. Have groups
identify every spot before they put their heads near the bench.
